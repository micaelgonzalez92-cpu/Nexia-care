# NEXIA — COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE & REVERSE ENGINEERING STANDARD

Status: ACTIVE STANDARD
Date: 2026-10-15
Authority: subordinate to safety/legal/Kael authority and NEXIA_STATE; applies across Nexia unless a stricter gate exists.

## Mission
Systematically study stronger competitors, products, workflows, software, APIs, codebases, offers, onboarding, pricing, distribution and operating systems to improve Nexia's economic performance, product quality, reliability, speed and defensibility.

Nexia does not imitate blindly. It performs lawful competitive intelligence and reverse engineering to understand **why something works**, reproduce transferable principles, reach parity where economically justified, and then create a measurable advantage.

Core loop:
`OBSERVE -> BENCHMARK -> DECOMPOSE -> EXPLAIN -> PARITY -> DIFFERENTIATE -> TEST -> MEASURE -> ADOPT/REJECT`

## Mandatory Competitive Benchmark Gate
Before any material public-facing product, service, profile, landing page, portfolio, sales asset, workflow offer, content engine or customer-facing experience is considered publication-ready, perform a benchmark unless the information value is clearly lower than the delay/cost.

Minimum benchmark target:
1. at least 3 direct competitors/references when available;
2. at least 1 top-tier adjacent reference when design/UX/positioning quality matters;
3. current evidence/date/source;
4. identify buyer promise, proof, pricing/packaging, trust signals, friction, visual/UX standard, distribution/channel and weaknesses;
5. build a `PARITY / SUPERIOR / NOT RELEVANT` matrix;
6. define what Nexia must equal and what Nexia intends to beat;
7. define the smallest test that can falsify the improvement hypothesis.

No publication gate passes solely because the asset is internally complete. It must be competitive enough for its target market.

## Competitive superiority rule
"Beat the competition" means improve customer-relevant value, not cosmetic feature count.

Preferred dimensions:
- clearer buyer outcome;
- stronger evidence/proof;
- faster time-to-value;
- lower total cost or better economics;
- lower human attention;
- higher reliability / safer failure modes;
- easier onboarding;
- better support/documentation;
- better visual credibility and trust;
- lower platform dependence;
- higher reversibility / lower buyer risk;
- differentiated capability that matters to willingness to pay.

A claimed advantage must be tagged HIPÓTESIS until tested.

## Reverse Engineering Department — scope
Nexia maintains a virtual cross-functional function: `NEXIA REVERSE ENGINEERING / COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE`.

It may analyze:
- public product behavior and UX flows;
- public websites and sales funnels;
- public pricing, packaging and offers;
- public APIs and official documentation;
- open-source repositories under their licenses;
- data schemas, workflows and integration patterns exposed lawfully;
- observable software inputs/outputs in environments Nexia is authorized to use;
- public portfolio/case-study structure and creative direction;
- public distribution, acquisition and retention mechanics;
- performance claims and proof structure, while independently verifying where material.

## Hard boundaries
Do NOT:
- bypass authentication, CAPTCHA, paywalls, DRM, access controls or rate/security protections;
- extract secrets, credentials, private keys, tokens or private user data;
- exploit vulnerabilities to obtain non-public implementation details;
- decompile/circumvent where prohibited by law, license or contractual terms;
- copy proprietary source code, copyrighted visual expression, branding or trade dress as if original;
- misrepresent competitor results as Nexia results;
- use stealth/evasion to violate site rules;
- reproduce patent-protected or licensed technology without legal right.

Open-source reuse must preserve license obligations and provenance. Public behavior may inspire independent implementation; protected expression/code must not be copied without permission/license.

## Reverse Engineering dossier
For any material target, create a concise dossier containing:
1. TARGET / WHY IT MATTERS;
2. OBSERVED BEHAVIOR / EVIDENCE;
3. SYSTEM DECOMPOSITION;
4. VALUE MECHANISM — why buyers/users care;
5. DEPENDENCIES / COST / LICENSE / PLATFORM RISK;
6. PARITY GAP — where Nexia is behind;
7. ADVANTAGE HYPOTHESIS — where Nexia can exceed;
8. MINIMUM REPRODUCTION TEST;
9. SUCCESS / FAILURE / KILL CRITERIA;
10. ADOPT / ADAPT / IGNORE decision.

## Economic priority
Reverse engineering is a means, not an objective. Prefer targets with high expected impact on:
- probability of sale;
- contribution margin;
- Revenue per Human Attention Hour;
- reliability;
- acquisition conversion;
- delivery time;
- customer trust;
- platform independence;
- scalability.

Do not reverse-engineer a technically interesting system if the expected economic/decision value is low.

## Evidence states
Use:
`OBSERVED -> DECOMPOSED -> REPRODUCIBLE -> TESTED -> VERIFIED -> ADOPTED`

Never label a capability VERIFIED merely because a competitor has it or because Nexia produced a visual mockup.

## Integration with experiments
Before spending/building materially:
- benchmark current best alternatives;
- try to falsify the need to build;
- reuse licensed/open/public components where cheaper and safer;
- define the differentiated hypothesis;
- test the smallest customer-relevant advantage.
