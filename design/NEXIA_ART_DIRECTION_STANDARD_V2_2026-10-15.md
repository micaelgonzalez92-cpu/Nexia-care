# NEXIA — ART DIRECTION STANDARD V2

Status: ACTIVE CREATIVE STANDARD
Date: 2026-10-15
Applies to: public-facing portfolio, landing pages, sales assets, product covers, case studies, content thumbnails and presentation hero visuals.

## Objective
Nexia visuals must not merely explain the system. They must create immediate commercial credibility while remaining truthful.

Priority hierarchy:
`BUYER OUTCOME -> VISUAL IDEA -> EVIDENCE -> TECHNOLOGY`

Never default to:
`TECH STACK -> DENSE DIAGRAM -> LOGS -> BUYER FIGURES IT OUT`.

## Pre-design benchmark gate
Before a material public asset:
1. inspect at least 3 relevant competitor/reference assets;
2. inspect at least 1 top-tier adjacent creative reference;
3. record what creates perceived quality: composition, art direction, typography, color, imagery, motion, mockups, hierarchy, narrative and proof;
4. identify generic patterns to avoid;
5. define the visual advantage target before generating assets.

## Quality bar
Target: premium case-study / senior creative-director standard, not generic AI/SaaS infographic output.

Every hero/cover should pass:
- recognisable focal idea in <2 seconds;
- readable at thumbnail/mobile size;
- one dominant message;
- distinctive composition;
- intentional negative space;
- typography used as a graphic element;
- coherent palette and texture;
- visual system consistent across the portfolio but not repetitive;
- no tiny fake UI details used as decoration;
- no invented metrics, testimonials, customer logos or production claims;
- no visual element that can reasonably be mistaken for real customer data unless it is clearly labelled synthetic/demo.

## Creative direction modes
Choose a mode based on the buyer/problem, not habit:
- editorial photography;
- premium product/UI mockup;
- abstract 3D/metaphor;
- kinetic/motion-first video;
- typographic campaign;
- system/flow illustration;
- mixed-media case-study collage.

Dark neon dashboards are only one option and must not become the default visual identity for every project.

## Case-study narrative
Recommended sequence:
1. COVER — outcome / tension / transformation;
2. PROBLEM — what goes wrong today;
3. SYSTEM — simplified solution architecture;
4. PROOF — tests/evidence/constraints;
5. DETAIL — technical implementation;
6. BOUNDARY — what is demo vs production;
7. CTA / service relevance.

A cover is an advertisement for the case study, not the case study itself.

## Copy rules for covers
Prefer 3–10 word commercial concepts.
Avoid paragraphs, logs and tables in the hero.

Current EXP-AUTO-001 concepts:
1. `FROM RAW LEADS TO REVIEW-READY DATA`
2. `ONE LEAD. ONE RECORD. NO DUPLICATE NOISE.`
3. `AUTOMATION THAT FAILS SAFELY`
4. `TEST BEFORE IT TOUCHES PRODUCTION`

Support line may name the capability/stack. `Internal demo / Technical proof` remains visible but subordinate.

## Visual QA scorecard — 100 points
- Buyer outcome clarity: 20
- Thumbnail/mobile impact: 15
- Art direction originality: 15
- Hierarchy/typography: 10
- Composition/negative space: 10
- Credibility/proof integrity: 10
- Competitive parity vs benchmark: 10
- Cross-portfolio coherence: 5
- Technical relevance: 5

Publication target: >=80/100 AND no truth-integrity failure.
If a top direct competitor still clearly looks more credible at a glance, asset remains REFINEMENT_REQUIRED unless economics/urgency justify shipping the weaker version as an explicit experiment.

## Asset versioning
- V1 = explanatory / technical documentation visual.
- V2 = commercial case-study visual.
- Supporting slides may contain technical diagrams/logs, but hero cover stays outcome-first.
