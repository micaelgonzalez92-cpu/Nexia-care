## Falsification notes
- Supplier claims of “100–250% return”, “sellable” or “popular” are marketing claims, not proof of completed sales, time-to-sale or net contribution.
- Do not compare €/kg to €/piece without item count, sellable yield and preparation time.
- For any future batch, record actual delivered cash cost, item-level condition, sellable status, cleaning/repair needs, photo/listing time, actual realized proceeds, platform/payment fees, shipping subsidy, packaging, returns and labor.
- For Vintage4Originals, preserve photos of every fault and timestamps. The public FAQ says B2B lots do not carry the consumer 14-day withdrawal right and claims must be made within 48 hours; remedy for excess faults is compensation on a later order. Verify current terms at receipt rather than relying only on indexed text.

## Current pilot economics (known costs only)
- Checkout total: €61.30 delivered / 10 acquired units = €6.13 per acquired unit.
- If 10/10 are sellable: allocated acquisition cost = €6.13 per sellable unit.
- If 8/10 are sellable: allocated acquisition cost = €7.6625 per sellable unit.
- At a €15 realized sale price, maximum contribution before all other costs is €8.87 per unit if 10 are sellable, or €7.3375 if only 8 are sellable.
- To reach €10 contribution **before fees, packaging, returns, labor and taxes**, price would need to be at least €16.13 with 10/10 sellable or €17.66 with 8/10 sellable.
- Therefore the existing €10 contribution criterion cannot be demonstrated at €15 using the known acquisition cost alone. Do not silently change the success threshold; Kael must decide whether to proceed with this learning pilot despite the economic warning or pause it.
